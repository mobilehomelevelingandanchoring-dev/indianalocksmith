**TITLE:** The Property Manager's Locksmith Checklist: How to Protect Your Orlando Investment Properties Between Tenants

**META DESCRIPTION:** Florida's high-turnover rental market makes between-tenant security a major liability risk. Here's the exact locksmith checklist every Orlando property manager should follow.

---

Orlando's rental market is relentless. A metro area with 300,000+ residents, 77 million annual tourists, one of the country's largest universities, and a Disney-adjacent workforce means property managers face one of the highest tenant turnover rates in the Southeast.

That constant churn creates a specific, underappreciated liability: key proliferation.

Every tenant who moves out potentially holds a copy of your property's key — or gave one to a roommate, partner, family member, or maintenance contact. After 3–5 tenant cycles, you genuinely don't know how many copies of your keys exist or who holds them.

Here's the locksmith checklist every Orlando property manager should follow — and why each step protects both your property and your legal standing.

---

## Step 1: Rekey Every Lock After Every Tenancy — Without Exception

This is the non-negotiable baseline. Florida landlord-tenant law (F.S. 83.51) doesn't explicitly mandate rekeying between tenants, but failure to do so creates significant liability exposure if a previous tenant's key is used to access the property.

**Rekeying vs. Replacing:** Rekeying reconfigures the lock cylinder to work with a new key pattern without replacing the full hardware. When the lock hardware is in good condition, rekeying is functionally identical to replacing — at roughly 40% of the cost.

**When to replace instead:**
- Lock hardware is more than 8–10 years old
- Visible wear, rust, or damage to the cylinder or bolt
- You're upgrading security grade
- You want to standardize all units to a master key system

For a portfolio of 10+ units, partnering with a reliable locksmith in Orlando on a standing service agreement typically reduces per-unit rekeying costs by 20–30%.

## Step 2: Audit the Full Entry Point Inventory

Most property managers rekey the front door and stop there. A complete entry point audit includes:

- **All exterior doors** (front, back, side, garage entry)
- **Sliding doors** (latch, anti-lift pins, secondary lock)
- **Garage door keypads** (change codes, not just locks)
- **Mailbox locks** (often forgotten, identity theft vector)
- **Storage unit locks** if applicable
- **Common area access** on multi-unit properties
- **Any smart lock systems** — change PIN codes and revoke app access

A tenant who keeps a copy of your garage keypad code has the same access as a tenant with a physical key.

---

## Step 3: Photograph and Document Lock Hardware on Move-In

Add a lock documentation section to your move-in checklist:
- Photograph all deadbolts, door knobs, sliding door hardware, and window locks
- Note the brand, grade, and condition of each
- Record the key serial number if the manufacturer provides one
- Have the tenant initial the lock documentation section

This protects you in deposit disputes if a tenant damages lock hardware, and provides a baseline for your next rekeying cycle.

---

## Step 4: Install Grade 1 Deadbolts on All Exterior Doors

Florida's insurance market is one of the most difficult in the country — carriers are actively looking for reasons to increase premiums or decline coverage. Properties with documented Grade 1 deadbolts on all exterior doors are increasingly preferred by insurers, and some carriers offer direct discounts.

Grade 3 locks (builder-grade) are standard in new Florida construction and are adequate for code compliance — not for security or insurance optimization.

---

## Step 5: Consider a Master Key System for Multi-Unit Properties

For portfolios of 4+ units in the same building or complex, a master key system allows you (or authorized maintenance) to access all units with a single key while each tenant retains exclusive access with their individual key.

Professional locksmith-installed master key systems include:
- Restricted key blanks (keys cannot be copied at hardware stores)
- Documented key control (you know exactly how many keys exist)
- Individual tenant lock-out capability without system-wide rekeying

This is particularly valuable for short-term rental properties and apartment complexes.

---

## Step 6: Add Smart Locks to High-Turnover or Short-Term Properties

For Airbnb, VRBO, or short-term rentals in Orlando, smart locks eliminate the key handoff problem entirely. Guests receive a unique PIN code that expires at checkout. You never need to be physically present for check-in or key collection.

**Implementation notes:**
- Always use a licensed locksmith for installation — smart lock setup errors can lock you out of your own property
- Choose a lock with a physical keypad override (battery failure is a real scenario in Florida heat)
- Integrate with your property management software if possible (many systems connect to Schlage, Yale, or August)

## The Liability Math

A single break-in at one of your units — due to an unreturned key from a previous tenant — costs:
- Insurance deductible: $1,000–$2,500
- Tenant property loss (potential lawsuit): $5,000–$25,000
- Emergency repairs: $500–$2,000
- Vacancy during investigation: $1,500–$5,000/month
- Legal fees if tenant sues: $5,000–$15,000

**Total exposure: $13,000–$49,500**

The cost of rekeying every unit between every tenancy: **$35–$65 per unit.**

The math is not complicated.
