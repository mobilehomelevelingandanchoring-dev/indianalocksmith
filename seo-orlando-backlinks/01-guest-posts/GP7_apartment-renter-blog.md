**TITLE:** Renter's Guide to Lock Security in Orlando: What Your Landlord Won't Tell You

**META DESCRIPTION:** Florida renters have specific rights around locks and security — and most don't know them. Here's what Orlando renters need to know about rekeying, lockouts, and protecting themselves.

Moving to Orlando is exciting — whether you're joining the UCF community, relocating for a theme park or hospitality job, or settling into one of the metro's fast-growing suburban neighborhoods. What nobody covers in the excitement of signing a lease: your lock security situation.

Your landlord is responsible for a lot. Your lock security — beyond the minimum — is largely on you.

---

## What Florida Law Actually Requires of Landlords

Florida Statute 83.51 outlines landlord obligations for residential property maintenance. On locks specifically, the law requires that landlords provide:

- **Deadbolt locks** on each exterior door that connects to the interior of the dwelling
- **Window locks** on windows designed to be opened
- **Peephole or door viewer** on the main exterior door

What Florida law does NOT require:
- Rekeying between tenancies (though many landlords do this)
- Grade 1 or Grade 2 deadbolts (the law doesn't specify security grade)
- Smart locks or electronic access
- Proof that previous tenant's keys have been recovered

**This means:** You may move into an apartment with a functional lock that has been used by multiple previous tenants — and their keys are still out there.

## What You Should Do On Move-In Day

### Request a Rekey — In Writing

As soon as your lease is signed, submit a written request to your landlord asking for confirmation that all locks have been rekeyed since the last tenant vacated. Email is sufficient — this creates a paper trail.

If your landlord confirms the rekey, ask for the date it was completed and the name of the locksmith or the documentation.

If your landlord does not confirm a rekey, you have two options:
1. Ask them to do it before you move in (they may agree or may not)
2. Hire a locksmith yourself and deduct the cost from rent (Florida allows deduction for essential services the landlord fails to provide — consult a tenant rights attorney for your specific situation)

### Do a Full Lock Assessment

On move-in day, before you sign the condition report:
- Test every exterior lock — does it operate smoothly? Any wobble in the cylinder?
- Check deadbolts — do they fully extend without resistance?
- Test all window locks
- Check the sliding door — does it lift out of the track? (A major vulnerability)
- Photograph everything and add notes to the condition report

### Change the Alarm Code If Applicable

If the apartment has a security system, request that the landlord change or reset the master code before move-in. If the system uses a PIN and the landlord gives you "the usual code," ask explicitly whether previous tenants had the same code.

## Know Your Rights During a Lockout

### What Landlords Can and Cannot Do

In Florida, a landlord cannot change your locks without proper legal process, even if you're behind on rent. Unauthorized lockout by a landlord (F.S. 83.67) is illegal and entitles the tenant to damages including attorney's fees.

If your landlord changes your locks without a court order, call the police and document the situation.

### What to Do When You're Locked Out

If you're locked out during business hours, you may be able to reach building management for a spare key. Outside business hours, you'll need a locksmith.

**Important:** Some leases include language that charges tenants for lockout calls to building management after hours. Read your lease carefully. A locksmith call — which typically costs $45–$85 for a standard apartment lockout — may be cheaper than a property management after-hours lockout fee.

## Security Upgrades Renters Can Make (Without Violating the Lease)

### Door Reinforcement Bar

A door reinforcement bar (also called a door security bar or "jammer") braces against the floor under the door handle and prevents the door from being kicked in — even if the deadbolt holds. It's removable, leaves no damage, and costs $25–$50.

### Portable Door Alarm

A small alarm that mounts in the door hinge area and triggers a loud alert when the door is opened. Battery-powered, removable, and excellent for ground-floor apartments.

### Sliding Door Charlies Bar

For apartments with sliding doors — extremely common in Florida — a Charlie bar or wooden dowel in the bottom track prevents the door from sliding open even if the latch is bypassed. Zero damage, zero installation required.

### Window Lock Pins

Angled pins inserted into the window frame prevent double-hung windows from being opened more than a few inches from outside. These are renter-safe: they require no installation tools and leave no damage.

### Smart Lock Adapter

If your lease permits, some smart lock models (like the August Smart Lock) attach to the interior thumb-turn of your existing deadbolt without replacing the lock hardware — meaning the landlord's key still works and no permanent modification occurs.

## Before You Move Out: What to Expect

When you give proper notice and vacate the property, your landlord is responsible for ensuring the unit is prepared for the next tenant. From a security standpoint, the cycle that started with your move-in rekey repeats.

If you made any lock modifications (with permission), document them and restore the original hardware during move-out. Failure to do so can result in deductions from your security deposit.
