**TITLE:** 9 Home Security Upgrades Every Florida Homeowner Should Make Before Hurricane Season

**META DESCRIPTION:** Florida homeowners face unique security risks beyond storms. Here are 9 locksmith-backed upgrades that protect your home year-round — and the ones professionals recommend first.

---

Florida homeowners spend weeks prepping for hurricane season — boarding windows, stocking supplies, checking flood zones. But one critical layer of home security gets overlooked almost every year: your locks.

Storms don't just damage property. They create opportunity. In the aftermath of major weather events, burglary rates spike in affected communities as criminals exploit displaced families, distracted law enforcement, and homes left temporarily vacant. Florida's unique geography — high humidity, intense UV, seasonal occupancy patterns — also accelerates lock wear in ways homeowners in other states don't encounter.

Here's what security professionals recommend before the season begins.

---

### 1. Replace Builder-Grade Locks With ANSI Grade 1 Deadbolts

The vast majority of Florida new construction uses Grade 3 deadbolts — the cheapest option that passes code inspection. These locks are rated for 150,000 cycles and minimal kick resistance.

ANSI Grade 1 deadbolts withstand 250,000+ cycles and significantly higher force loads. For a single exterior door, an upgrade typically costs $80–$150 installed. For a home with three exterior entries, that's a $250–$400 investment that dramatically changes your risk profile.

Ask your locksmith to show you the grade certification on the packaging before installation.

---

### 2. Reinforce Strike Plates — The Part That Actually Fails

This is the single most overlooked security upgrade in residential locksmithing. In 95% of forced entry cases, the door lock itself does not fail — the strike plate pulls out of the doorframe.

Standard strike plates use ½-inch screws into soft wood. A reinforced box strike plate uses 3-inch screws that reach the structural stud behind the frame. This upgrade costs under $30 in hardware and 20 minutes of installation time.

If you only do one thing on this list, do this.

---

### 3. Rekey Before and After Storm Evacuations

Many Florida families evacuate during severe storms — sometimes for days or weeks. During that time, keys may be left with neighbors, property managers, or emergency contacts.

Rekeying after you return resets access without the cost of full lock replacement. It takes a licensed locksmith under an hour for a typical home and costs $25–$45 per lock cylinder. This is especially important in communities with high seasonal rental turnover.

---

### 4. Upgrade Sliding Door Locks

Sliding glass doors are the most commonly exploited entry point in Florida home break-ins. The standard latch that comes with most sliding doors can be defeated by lifting the door off its track.

A proper security upgrade includes:
- **A floor bolt** or Charlie bar that prevents the door from sliding open
- **Anti-lift pins** in the top track to prevent the door from being lifted out
- **A secondary keyed lock** for high-risk properties

Many homeowners invest in smart locks for their front door while leaving an unsecured sliding door 15 feet away.

---

### 5. Install Smart Locks With Professional Setup

Smart locks offer genuine security benefits — audit trails, remote access, temporary codes for service workers, and automatic locking. But installation errors eliminate these benefits entirely.

The most common mistake: installing a smart lock on a door with a weak strike plate or hollow core. The smart lock records who entered — but the door can still be kicked in.

A licensed locksmith ensures your smart lock installation includes:
- Door and hardware compatibility assessment
- Frame reinforcement if needed
- Battery backup protocols
- Correct bore hole sizing

### 6. Add Window Key Locks to Ground-Floor Windows

Florida building codes require windows to open for egress in bedrooms, but this creates entry points. Keyed window locks allow windows to be cracked for ventilation while preventing them from being fully opened from outside.

This is particularly important for homes near vacation corridors, rental districts, or anywhere with significant transient foot traffic.

---

### 7. Install a Peephole or Video Doorbell at Every Entry Point

Door-to-door crime often begins with someone confirming occupancy — or lack thereof. A video doorbell at the front and back entry points deters approach, documents visitors, and lets you verify callers without opening the door.

Install options include traditional wide-angle peepholes (ideal for renters in buildings that prohibit modifications) and full video doorbells with cloud recording.

---

### 8. Have a Locksmith Assess Your Garage Entry Door

The door connecting your garage to your living space is frequently the weakest interior door in the home — often hollow-core, often without a deadbolt, and rarely reinforced. If your garage door opens, that interior door is the only barrier between an intruder and your living space.

This door should meet the same standard as an exterior door: solid core construction, Grade 1 deadbolt, reinforced strike plate. Many homeowners are surprised to find their garage entry door has neither a deadbolt nor a reinforced frame.

---

### 9. Create a Household Emergency Lockout Plan

This isn't a hardware recommendation — it's a protocol one. Every household should have:
- A trusted neighbor who holds a spare key
- A saved contact for a 24/7 local locksmith
- A plan for what to do if locked out with children or pets in summer heat

## Final Thought

Home security is a system, not a product. A $500 smart lock on a hollow door with a builder-grade strike plate is less secure than a $80 Grade 1 deadbolt properly installed on a reinforced frame.
