## The Problem Is Real — And Florida Is a Target

The Federal Trade Commission has issued multiple consumer alerts about locksmith scams concentrated in high-tourism states. Florida — specifically the Orlando, Miami, and Tampa markets — appears on the FTC's radar for a reason: millions of visitors in unfamiliar areas, distracted and stressed, searching for help on a smartphone.

The Better Business Bureau's 2024 Scam Tracker identified locksmith fraud as one of the top 10 service-industry scams by dollar volume. The average victim loses between $150 and $450 above the original quoted price.

## How the Locksmith Scam Works (Step by Step)

**Step 1 — The Fake Listing**
Scammers create dozens of fake business listings on Google Maps, Yelp, and Bing. The listings use stock photos, fake reviews (often posted in bursts within short windows), and a local phone number that routes to a call center.

**Step 2 — The Bait Quote**
When you call, the "dispatcher" quotes an unrealistically low price — typically $25–$45 for any lockout. This is the bait.

**Step 3 — The Subcontract**
The call center dispatches the nearest available contractor — often unlicensed, sometimes operating without insurance, and with no connection to the "business" you called. You don't know this is happening.

**Step 4 — The Price Switch**
The contractor arrives and assesses the situation. Then the excuses begin: "Your lock is a high-security model," "This requires drilling," "There's an extra charge for after-hours." The final invoice is 3–10x the original quote. You're at your car or home, the contractor has the leverage, and paying feels like the only option.

**Step 5 — The Pressure**
Many scam operators use high-pressure tactics: refusing to leave, threatening to call the police if you won't pay, claiming the lock is now "drilled out" and must be replaced at $300+. Some physically block access to the lock until payment is made.

## Red Flags — How to Spot a Fake Locksmith Listing

### On the Phone
- ❌ Refuses to give a specific price before dispatch
- ❌ Cannot provide a Florida locksmith license number immediately
- ❌ Local-sounding name but the phone number traces to a different state
- ❌ Extreme urgency pressure ("We only have one tech left near you")
- ❌ Overly generic business name ("Local Locksmith 24/7," "City Locksmith Service")

### On Google/Yelp
- ❌ Reviews are all 5-star with no detail ("Great service! Very fast!")
- ❌ All reviews posted within the same 2–4 week window
- ❌ No photos of actual technicians or the business location
- ❌ Address is a UPS Store, virtual office, or residential area
- ❌ Business has been listed for less than 6 months with 50+ reviews
- ❌ No responses to negative reviews

### When They Arrive
- ❌ Arrives in an unmarked vehicle with no branding
- ❌ Cannot show a Florida locksmith license when asked
- ❌ Immediately claims "drilling is necessary" before attempting non-destructive entry
- ❌ Refuses to provide a written quote before starting work
- ❌ Price is significantly higher than the phone quote without a clear explanation

## The Legal Test — What Florida Law Requires

Florida Statute Chapter 493 regulates locksmith licensing in the state. Every locksmith operating in Florida is required to:

1. Hold a current Florida Class D (security officer) or Class B (recovery/repo) license, or a specific locksmith registration through the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services
2. Display their license number upon request
3. Carry proof of insurance

**Verify any locksmith instantly:** Visit [myfloridalicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com) and search by name or license number.

If a locksmith cannot show their license — or gives a number that doesn't verify — stop the interaction immediately and call the non-emergency police line.

## What to Do If You've Been Scammed

1. **Document everything:** Photographs of the vehicle/truck, photos of any invoice, the technician's appearance, the time of arrival
2. **Get the license plate number** of their vehicle
3. **File with the FTC:** reportfraud.ftc.gov
4. **File with the Florida AG:** myfloridalegal.com
5. **File with the BBB:** bbb.org/scamtracker
6. **File a Google Maps report:** Flag the listing as fraudulent — this helps others
7. **Dispute the charge with your credit card:** If you paid by card, document the bait-and-switch pricing and initiate a dispute

## How to Find a Legitimate Locksmith in Florida

A legitimate Florida locksmith will:
- ✅ Provide a license number before dispatch
- ✅ Give a firm or binding price quote on the phone
- ✅ Arrive in a branded vehicle or with ID
- ✅ Show their license immediately upon request
- ✅ Attempt non-destructive entry before recommending drilling
- ✅ Provide a written invoice that matches the phone quote
- ✅ Not add surcharges after the work is complete
